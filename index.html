Renters can now request installation under the Renters' Rights Act 2025 — landlords cannot unreasonably refuse.

Yes — as of 15 April 2026. BS 7671 Amendment 4 legalised sub-800W plug-in solar systems connected via a standard UK socket. You must still complete a free DNO registration, but no electrician is required. All products on PluginDirect are UK-certified and compliant.
In most cases, no planning permission is required. Plug-in solar kits mounted on a balcony rail, south-facing wall, or garden frame are treated as permitted development. Listed buildings and conservation areas may require separate consent — check with your local planning authority.
A typical 800W kit saves between £160 and £220 per year in the UK, depending on your location and usage habits. South-facing in London saves more than north-facing in Scotland. Adding battery storage can increase savings by a further 20–40%. Use our savings calculator for a personalised figure.
Yes. Under the Renters' Rights Act 2025, tenants can formally request to install plug-in solar and landlords cannot unreasonably refuse. Since plug-in solar requires no drilling, structural changes, or roof access, the threshold for "reasonable" is very low. Put your request in writing and reference the Act. Read our full renters' guide →
Most homeowners complete the full setup — mounting the panel, connecting the micro-inverter, plugging in, and completing DNO registration — in 90 minutes to 2 hours. No specialist tools are needed. EcoFlow and Anker kits include everything in the box, including mounting hardware.
800W (2 panels) is the right choice for most households — it's the legal maximum and doubles your savings for typically 60–80% more upfront cost. A 400W single-panel kit is a good starting point for flats with limited balcony space, or if you want to trial the technology before committing. Use our comparison tool to filter by system size.
Not essential, but highly recommended if you're out during the day. Without a battery, electricity generated while you're away is exported to the grid for little return. Adding a battery (£350–£680) shifts generation to evening use and can boost your annual saving by 25–40%. If you work from home, panels-only gives the fastest payback.
DNO stands for Distribution Network Operator — the company that manages the electricity grid in your area. UK law requires you to notify your DNO before connecting any generation device to the grid. It's free, takes around 5 minutes online, and there's no approval wait. Find your DNO by entering your postcode at energynetworks.org →
It shouldn't, as long as you've completed DNO registration and the system complies with BS 7671 Amendment 4. Notify your insurer before installation — most will simply note it on your policy. Unregistered systems connected before the regulation changed (pre-April 2026) did risk voiding cover.
Excess electricity flows back into the grid. Under the Smart Export Guarantee (SEG), your energy supplier must offer you a tariff for this export — rates range from 4p to 25p per kWh depending on your supplier. Without battery storage, about 40–50% of what you generate may be exported during low-usage periods.
Based on our testing, the top brands for UK buyers in 2026 are: EcoFlow (best all-round, government partner), Anker SOLIX (best built-in battery), Zendure (best smart-home integration), Marstek (best compact kit), and Hoymiles (fastest payback for DIY builders). Yes — solar panels generate electricity from daylight, not direct sunshine. A UK 800W system typically generates 30–40% of its summer output in winter. Output on an overcast day is lower but not zero. South-facing panels at a 30–35° tilt give the best year-round performance in the UK. Modern high-efficiency cells (such as Anker's 25% efficiency panels) perform better in low-light conditions.
Yes — this is one of the biggest advantages over rooftop solar. Plug-in solar kits are fully portable. Unplug, unmount, pack, and reinstall at your new address. You'll need to complete a new DNO registration at the new address, which takes 5 minutes. This makes them ideal for renters or anyone who moves frequently.
They serve different markets. Rooftop solar (3–5kWp) generates 10–20× more electricity and has far better long-term returns if you own your home and have a suitable roof. Plug-in solar is the right choice if you rent, live in a flat, can't access your roof, or want a low-cost entry point (£200–£1,000 vs £6,000+). Use our guide to compare both options →
There is currently no direct grant for plug-in solar kits in the UK. However, plug-in solar is VAT-exempt (0% VAT applies to all solar equipment in the UK since April 2022). You can also benefit from the Smart Export Guarantee for any electricity you export. Some local councils are exploring plug-in solar as part of fuel poverty schemes — check with your council.
Most kits include a 5-year product warranty on the micro-inverter and 10–12 years on the panels themselves (with a 25-year performance guarantee on output levels). EcoFlow and Anker both offer 5-year warranties. Thunder Energy offers 3 years. Always buy from an authorised UK distributor to ensure warranty claims are honoured without costly international returns.
The BSI product standard is a kit certification framework expected in July 2026. Once published, manufacturers can submit their kits for testing and certification. A BSI-certified kit will confirm the plug, cable, and inverter meet UK safety specifications. Important: BS 7671 Amendment 4 already legalised the installation method in April 2026 — the BSI standard is additional product certification, not a gate to legality. All kits on PluginDirect are already legal to install. The BSI standard will make the self-install route clearer for new kits designed to that specification.
BS 7671 Amendment 4 requires Type A RCD protection on any circuit that has plug-in solar connected. Most consumer units installed after 2010 already include Type A RCDs. Older properties may have Type AC RCDs — these were not designed for bidirectional power flow and may need upgrading. Before buying a kit, locate your consumer unit (fuse box), check the label on the RCD switch, and use our RCD check tool above. Upgrading an individual RCD typically costs £50–£120 from a CPS-registered electrician.
Yes, via the Smart Export Guarantee (SEG). All licensed UK energy suppliers with over 150,000 customers must offer SEG tariffs. Typical rates: Octopus Energy 4p/kWh, British Gas 7p/kWh, E.ON Next 15p/kWh. To receive SEG payments you need a smart meter and must register with your supplier. Without battery storage, roughly 40–50% of what an 800W system generates during daylight may be exported if you're not home. Use our advanced calculator to model SEG income for your specific scenario.
